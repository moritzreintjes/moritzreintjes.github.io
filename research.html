Research program

My research concerns geometric regularity and singularities in General Relativity, nonlinear PDE in relativistic fluid dynamics, and mathematical quantum physics.

Singular geometries, essential regularity and the RT-equations

A central problem in geometry and General Relativity is to determine whether a singularity is an artefact of a coordinate representation or an intrinsic true breakdown of the geometry. Together with Blake Temple, I developed a general theory to address this question for geometries of low-regularity described by affine connections. The theory identifies the essential regularity of a connection—its highest possible regularity under coordinate transformation—and gives a checkable criterion for when that regularity has been reached. It also provides a constructive procedure for removing a singularity by regularizing the connection to its essential regularity.

The analytic mechanism is provided by the RT-equations, (Regularity Transformation equations), a nonlinear elliptic system that determines regularizing coordinate transformations. Their elliptic character is independent of metric signature, which is particularly striking in General Relativity, whose field equations are fundamentally hyperbolic. The RT-equations raise connection regularity relative to curvature and, through an additional hidden regularization of curvature, can be iterated to reach the essential regularity. The theory works locally and globally and applies, in particular, to singularities at general-relativistic shock waves and to cusp-type singularities that obstruct numerical simulation.

Extending the theory of the RT-equations from tangent to vector bundles led to an extension of Uhlenbeck compactness from Riemannian geometry to Lorentzian and more general non-Riemannian settings, and from compact to non-compact gauge groups. This also extends classical regularity results of Kazdan–DeTurck to connections on vector bundles. The RT-equations led to several applications in General Relativity, ranging from Penrose's Strong Cosmic Censorship conjecture, to an existence theory for geodesic equations, to the Hawking and Penrose singularity theorems at low regularity.

Relativistic Fluid Dynamics

Shock Waves are singular solutions of the compressible Euler equations of fluid dynamics. My group and I focus on various aspects related to shock waves, mostly in the context of Relativity. One theme are relativistic stellar phase transitions, for which we developed a general existence theory for static spherically symmetric solutions of the Einstein–Euler equations that allows an arbitrary number of such phase transitions. The construction is based on an extension of the Buchdahl condition to general discontinuous density profiles. This work is motivated in part by questions of gravitational collapse.

A persistent difficulty in relativistic fluid dynamics is the construction of dissipative or viscous Navier-Stokes-type approximations that remain compatible with Lorentz invariance. We introduced and analyzed a particularly simple Lorentz-invariant model for viscous approximations of relativistic shock waves, and established causality and dissipativity of the flow, as well as the existence of shock profiles and global-in-time existence of smooth solutions. We also study related dissipation models and problems of classical shock wave theory, and are interested in applications of such models.

Mathematical Quantum Physics

In mathematical quantum theory, I have worked extensively with Felix Finster, and more recently with Niky Kamran and Claudio Dappiaggi, on Causal Fermion Systems, a measure-theoretic approach to fundamental physics and quantum gravity. Our recent work addresses how quantum states can be extracted from a causal fermion system and how their dynamics can be described. This is related to my earlier work with Finster on the fermionic signature operator, which provides a non-perturbative method for distinguishing positive- and negative-energy states in time-dependent spacetimes or external fields, where the usual frequency decomposition is no longer available.
